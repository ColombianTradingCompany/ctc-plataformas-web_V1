The Coffee Varieties Map — Colombian edition

An extended, interactive edition of the coffee genealogy poster, issued by Colombian Trading Company. It keeps the original global genealogy intact and adds what that map leaves out: Colombia.

Why this edition exists

The World Coffee Research varieties catalog — the source of the underlying genealogy — carries no entry for Castillo, Variedad Colombia, Cenicafé 1 or Tabi. In a country where the FNC reports over 90% of planted area in rust-resistant varieties, that means essentially all of Colombia's working genetics were missing from the map.

What was added

  • The Cenicafé lineage — Variedad Colombia (1982), Castillo (2005), the three Castillo zonal composites (2018), Cenicafé 1 (2016) and Castillo 2.0 (2024), drawn from the shared Caturra × Timor Hybrid 1343 breeding population rather than as separate crosses.
  • Tabi (2002), Colombia's tall large-bean variety for shade systems.
  • The Andean landrace family — Chiroso, Pink Bourbon, Ombligón, Papayo, Bourbon Ají, Wush Wush, Sidra, Typica Mejorado and San Bernardo. These carry Bourbon, Typica or Caturra names that DNA testing does not support; testing keeps returning Ethiopian landrace ancestry instead. Drawn with dashed links because WCR treats the grouping as unverified pending systematic testing.
  • A full profile for every entry on the map — etymology, type, history and origin with a located map pin, ideal altitude band, grain size and common cup notes, plus a lineage diagram, all exportable as a PDF.

Where the information is thin, it says so

Individual profiles are written for the 95 arabica entries. The 57 canephora clones are breeding-programme codes with almost no published record, so each inherits an honest profile for its genetic group rather than being given invented tasting notes. Entries with no published cup record simply omit that section.

Sources

Genealogy and agronomic descriptions follow the World Coffee Research Varieties Catalog. Colombian material follows Cenicafé's Avances Técnicos 300 (Tabi), 337 (Castillo), 469 (Cenicafé 1), 489 (Castillo zonales) and 566 (Castillo 2.0), together with FNC figures and published variety genetics. Landrace attributions come from commercial DNA fingerprinting and are flagged as provisional wherever they appear.
